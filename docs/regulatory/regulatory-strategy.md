# Regulatory Strategy — Working Document

## Status

This document records product-development questions. It is not legal or regulatory advice.

## Ireland/EU first

Regulatory design should begin from the intended Irish/EU launch context rather than defaulting to U.S. healthcare assumptions.

## Medical Device Regulation

A formal opinion should be obtained early on whether proposed functions fall within EU medical-device software requirements.

Particular attention should be paid to future features that:

- provide information to clinicians;
- highlight items for review;
- stratify risk;
- influence diagnosis or therapy;
- generate patient-specific recommendations.

Do not assume that calling something a “support tool” keeps it outside MDR. Intended purpose and actual functionality matter.

## EU AI Act

The AI Act workstream should assess the obligations relevant to the product as functionality becomes concrete. Avoid prematurely assigning a final classification in marketing materials.

## Regulatory architecture implications

The regulatory decision may affect:

- intended-purpose wording;
- data model;
- audit logs;
- model/version traceability;
- quality management;
- human oversight;
- validation;
- post-market monitoring;
- change control.

## 30-day action

Before building clinical decision-support functionality:

1. prepare a one-page feature/intended-purpose matrix;
2. obtain qualified EU/Ireland medical-device regulatory advice;
3. decide whether the first clinical product is intentionally non-device or follows a medical-device pathway;
4. update this document and architecture accordingly.

## Claims

No CE marking, conformity, MDR class or regulatory status may be claimed until formally established.
