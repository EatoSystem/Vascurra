# Consent, Capacity & Control

## Design principle

Cognitive change must not automatically transfer control from a person to family members, caregivers or software.

## Core principles

1. **Presume capacity.** Do not infer incapacity from age, diagnosis, app behaviour or a cognitive score.
2. **Decision-specific support.** Support understanding and choice rather than treating capacity as all-or-nothing.
3. **Explain data use.** People should understand what is collected, why and who can see it.
4. **Explicit family access.** Family permissions should be granted intentionally, not assumed.
5. **Visible permissions.** The person should be able to see who has access and to what.
6. **Revocation.** Access should be revocable where law and current decision-support arrangements permit.
7. **Auditability.** Material access and permission changes should be recorded.
8. **Supported transition.** If decision-making arrangements change, the product must follow documented legal authority rather than silently changing ownership or access.

## Ireland-first review

The future product should be reviewed against Irish supported-decision-making law and guidance before implementing capacity-related workflows.

## Product implication

Consent and capacity are not “settings screens”. They are foundational domain models that affect:

- identity;
- sharing;
- family access;
- clinician access;
- research consent;
- data export/deletion;
- future account recovery;
- emergency exceptions.

## Phase 1

The website should explain the philosophy without collecting health information or implementing capacity workflows.
