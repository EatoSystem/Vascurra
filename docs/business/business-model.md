# Business Model

## Principle

Vascurra should build a sustainable company without creating incentives for surveillance, fear-based selling or exploitation of health data.

## Recommended model: staged hybrid

### 1. Personal subscription

Potential future value:

- everyday support;
- voice companion;
- personal organisation;
- longitudinal review;
- appointment preparation.

Commercial hypothesis only. Pricing should be tested after utility is validated.

### 2. Family plan

Potential future value:

- care-circle access;
- coordination;
- family summaries;
- permissions;
- practical support.

A family plan may be a stronger early willingness-to-pay proposition than individual-only pricing because multiple people receive value.

### 3. Clinical / memory-clinic licensing

Potential future model:

- per-clinician subscription;
- per-practice licence;
- clinic/site licence;
- enterprise health-system agreement.

Value proposition must be demonstrated through time saved, better information flow, patient/family experience or other validated outcomes.

### 4. Research partnerships

Potential future revenue/funding:

- sponsored research infrastructure;
- approved study tooling;
- data-collection services;
- analytics on appropriately governed/de-identified datasets;
- grant-funded collaborations.

Research data must never be quietly monetised outside consent/governance.

### 5. Enterprise / payer / employer pathways

Possible later model for prevention and vascular cognitive health programmes, but this should not become a Phase 1 priority.

## Recommended commercial sequence

### Phase 0
Founder-funded discovery and non-dilutive support.

### Phase 1
Public website, partnerships, grants and design-partner recruitment.

### Phase 2
Small paid or grant-funded pilots with families/clinicians/research groups.

### Phase 3
Validate recurring Personal/Family and Clinical revenue models.

### Phase 4
Health-system and research scale.

## Business-model guardrails

Do not build incentives around:

- engagement for engagement’s sake;
- unnecessary notifications;
- selling identifiable health data;
- escalating anxiety to increase conversion;
- clinical claims before evidence.

## Metrics to test later

- willingness to pay;
- retention because of genuine utility;
- family burden reduction;
- clinician time saved;
- conversion from design partner to paid deployment;
- support cost per household;
- gross margin after AI/voice costs;
- regulatory/compliance cost by product tier.
